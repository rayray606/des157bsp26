Project Overview

The Future of Korean Pastor's Kids is an interactive web experience that explores how church culture, family expectations, and mental health support may shape the future of Korean pastor's kids. The project was inspired by research showing that Korean Americans often experience barriers to mental health support despite significant mental health needs.

My target audience includes Korean church members, pastor's families, young adults, and anyone interested in understanding how cultural and religious expectations influence mental health. The goal of the project is to encourage reflection on current challenges while allowing users to explore possible future outcomes through speculative design. By presenting different futures, users are invited to consider what actions churches and communities can take today to create healthier environments for future generations.

Project Research

One of the most important stages of the project was conducting comparative research. I analyzed The Trevor Project and Mental Health America to understand how mental health organizations communicate difficult topics through storytelling, visual hierarchy, and accessible resources.

The Trevor Project demonstrated how personal stories and emotional design can create empathy, while Mental Health America showed how information and resources can be organized in a clear and educational format. This research helped me shift my project from simply presenting information to creating an interactive experience that combines storytelling, data visualization, and user reflection. The research also reinforced the importance of balancing emotional engagement with factual information.

Visual Design

The visual design evolved throughout the development process. Early sketches focused primarily on displaying information, but feedback suggested that the project needed stronger visual engagement and interaction. I selected a purple and blue color palette because it creates a reflective and supportive atmosphere while maintaining readability. Animated gradients created with Granim.js help establish an emotional tone from the moment users enter the website. I also used card-based layouts, whitespace, and simple typography to improve readability and reduce cognitive overload. Through revisions, I simplified several design elements to create a cleaner and more professional experience.

User Interface

The user interface was designed to guide users through a narrative journey. Users begin by learning about the current reality facing many Korean pastor's kids before exploring future scenarios. Interactive buttons allow users to compare a future without change and a future with support, encouraging them to think critically about possible outcomes. Data visualizations created with Chart.js communicate research findings about mental health needs and barriers to therapy. A reflection section allows users to actively participate by sharing their own thoughts.

These interactions were designed using principles of visibility, feedback, and simplicity to reduce cognitive load and create a clear user experience.

  1. Explore both future scenarios.
  2. Review the chart and consider its message.
  3. Write a short reflection.
  4. Navigate the site naturally and note any confusion.

Revisions

User testing provided valuable feedback about both the content and usability of the project. Testers responded positively to the future scenarios but suggested adding more information about the current reality and including stronger connections between research and the interactive elements. Based on this feedback, I expanded the Current Reality section, added additional data visualizations, and improved the descriptions of the future outcomes. I also implemented a usability testing overlay that provided clear instructions and improved navigation for first-time users. If I continued developing the project, I would add additional future scenarios, more interactive storytelling elements, and a larger collection of research-based data.

Summary

This project taught me how to combine research, visual design, and interactive web development to communicate a complex social issue. I learned the importance of user testing and how feedback can significantly improve both content and usability. I am most proud of transforming academic research into an interactive experience that encourages reflection and discussion. The most challenging part of the project was finding reliable research specifically related to Korean pastor's kids and translating that research into meaningful interactions. The most rewarding part was creating a project that connects design with an issue that is personally meaningful and socially relevant.
