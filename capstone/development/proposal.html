Capstone Proposal: Korean Pastor Kids and Mental Health

My capstone project will explore the mental health experiences of Korean pastor’s kids, especially how cultural expectations, church community pressure, and family roles can shape their identity and emotional well-being. Pastor’s kids are often expected to be role models, act “perfect,” and represent their parents’ ministry, even when they are struggling privately. I want this project to ask: What future are Korean church communities creating for pastor’s kids if mental health stays hidden, and what future could exist if support, boundaries, and honest conversations became normal?

The project could become an interactive web experience where users move through different possible futures. One path could show a dystopian future where silence, shame, and pressure continue, leading to burnout, isolation, or people leaving the church. Another path could show a more hopeful future where Korean churches create safe mental health conversations, counseling resources, support groups, and healthier boundaries for pastor families. The project might include anonymous survey questions, clickable story paths, data visualization, and reflective prompts where users can compare their own assumptions about church, family, faith, and mental health.

My target audience has three levels of specificity. Broadly, this project is for young people who grew up in religious or cultural communities with strong expectations. More specifically, it is for Korean American Christian students, church members, and second-generation Korean youth who may understand the pressure of balancing culture, faith, and identity. Most specifically, it is for Korean pastor’s kids and people close to them, including parents, church leaders, and peers who may not fully understand the hidden emotional pressure pastor’s kids experience.

The main lenses for this project are speculative design, challenging mental models, data visualization, and utopian/dystopian narratives. Speculative design will help imagine what Korean church communities could look like in 5, 10, or 15 years. Challenging mental models will question ideas like “pastor’s kids should always be strong” or “mental health struggles mean weak faith.” Data visualization could show survey responses or research about mental health stigma in Asian American and religious communities. Utopian and dystopian narratives will help users compare a future where nothing changes with a future where churches become more supportive and emotionally honest.
