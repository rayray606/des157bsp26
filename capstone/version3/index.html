The Future of Korean Pastor's Kids

Exploring how faith, culture, and mental health shape possible futures for Korean pastor's kids.

Current Reality

Many Korean pastor's kids grow up carrying pressure from both family and church community expectations. Because their parent is a church leader, they may feel expected to act as role models, participate in church life, and protect their family's reputation. Research on Korean church communities shows that stigma,shame, and cultural expectations can make mental health difficult to discuss openly.

Gloria Cho's research on Korean pastors explains that Korean churches can provide social support and cultural connection,but pastors may also feel limited in their ability to address mental health concerns. The Springer article also connects religion and mental health by showing how faith can support people emotionally, while stigma can prevent people from seeking help.

The Erasing Shame podcast and Sola Network interview add more personal and community-based perspectives. They show that pastor's kids and teenagers in Christian communities may struggle with perfectionism, identity, legalism, and the pressure to hide emotional pain. This project uses those sources to ask what kind of future Korean churches are creating: one where pastor's kids continue to struggle silently, or one where mental health support,honesty, and care become part of church culture.

Mental Health Factors

Research shows a significant gap between mental health needs and the use of professional support among Asian Americans and Korean Americans. Cultural stigma, lack of awareness, cost, and the desire to "save face" can prevent individuals from seeking help.
